# MyBarPool FAQ

**Every attempt before this was one grid, one Super Bowl, one person holding the money. MyBarPool is every game, every week, with the money held by a program nobody can override.**

The rules are in the [README](../README.md), the reasoning in [ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md) and the exact mechanics in [PROGRAM.md](PROGRAM.md). This page answers the questions people ask before they read those, starting with the one everyone asks first.

## Hasn't this been tried before?

Many times, since the first bitcoin forums, and the history is short enough to tell. Names are left out; the pattern is the point.

- **2014.** A bitcoin casino added a grid for the Super Bowl as a side feature of its sportsbook. Its boards filled, and a shareholder on the forum wished another were open; the company rebranded within the year and the grid stayed a once-a-year feature. The same month a forum member published a small PHP app he had written for his own party: people pre-registered online, he printed the grid on game day and assigned the numbers by hand.
- **2016.** A forum thread ran a 100-box grid at 0.001 BTC a box, then cut the price to 0.0002 BTC to spark interest. Two members held the money as escrow and kept the log by hand under the table. The thread's own conclusion was that it needed a provably fair way to assign boxes.
- **2018.** A Monero pool for Super Bowl LII: 100 boxes at 0.02 XMR, positions drawn from block hashes after a deadline, one player underpaid, the winner paid by hand, and a closing line hoping for more players next year. There was no next year. Ethereum's first contracts appeared the same season: one-game outcome bets with a hand-rolled oracle in which anyone staking ETH could vote on the result.
- **2019–2020.** Ethereum grid contracts with an oracle for the scores. Their own READMEs worried about rounding and about the contract being spammed; one course-project contract reached mainnet, had its public RPC key abused, and now looks dead. Every one of them was built for a single game.
- **2022–2023.** The oracle era. Grids with a verifiable random function for the digits and an oracle network's sports data for the scores, sold as NFTs. A CosmWasm grid in which the creator started the game and chose the winner by hand, keeping 10% of the prize pool. An Algorand grid whose boxes were NFTs with the digits already shuffled onto them, so buyers saw their numbers before kickoff; one buyer wrote that it would be a boring game if his turned out to be the final score. A site still running today draws its axes with a VRF, has the scores submitted on-chain, pays automatically with rollovers, and takes no fee, earning yield on the pot while it waits. One grid a year, all of them.
- **2024 onward, on Solana.** A grid built in under two weeks by a first-time Solana developer: a 6.9% fee, winners claiming one of five payouts, the scores typed in by the operator, and by its author's own account never reviewed. It worked for one Super Bowl. A developer community ran its own grid at a fixed buy-in with four fixed payouts, also for one game.

Separately, several well-funded teams built peer-to-peer sports exchanges on-chain, with order books and shared liquidity, and their hard problem was never the chain: an order book needs market makers before it needs players. A grid does not. It needs 25 people.

### What the history teaches

1. **One event, one organiser, no second game.** Every attempt was Super Bowl only, so nothing held players between Februaries and the builder had moved on by March. MyBarPool opens a grid on every NFL game as soon as the week's kickoff times are published, which is a product with a next game, not an event.
2. **Filling the grid was what killed them, not the technology.** Prices were cut to spark interest, and a 100-box grid is a hard sell among strangers. The MyBarPool grid is 25 boxes, which a bar table fills; the creator can seed it with up to 5 boxes; anyone can add to the prizes as a sponsor; private pools keep a bar's grid among its regulars; and every pool is one link away.
3. **Trust was always a person.** A forum escrow, a deployer who chose the winner, an operator who typed the scores. The only projects that removed the person did it with an oracle network and still had a person picking the game. MyBarPool keeps an operator, the keeper, and binds it: it can only pay the box the posted score names, scores go in quarter order and cannot be rushed, two independent sources must agree before a score is posted, and every return and split follows a rule written in the program. Who holds which keys, and what each can and cannot do, is in the README.
4. **Randomness was never solved.** Block hashes after a deadline, a vote, a VRF, or giving up and pre-shuffling the digits. MyBarPool's digits come from a commit-reveal program whose commit is recorded on the pool before the end slot and verified at the draw, so the result depends on nothing the operator can choose after the fact, and anyone can re-derive it. See [Randomness](../README.md#randomness).
5. **Claiming loses money.** Where winners had to claim, forgotten wallets and unclaimed payouts followed. MyBarPool pushes every prize to the winner's wallet in the settlement transaction itself. Nobody claims.
6. **Nobody could be found.** Wallet-gated web pages, discovered through a forum post or a tweet, with no mobile app and no way to share a grid. MyBarPool ships on the Solana Seeker through the dApp Store and on the web, every pool has one link that reads straight from the chain, and the program and SDK are open for other clients to build on.
7. **Nobody was reviewed.** "Never had a code review", a leaked RPC key, a README worried about rounding. MyBarPool's program is built reproducibly, tested against its specification, fuzzed, linted with the Solana-specific lints, and independently audited before the first mainnet deploy, with the report published.

## Questions

**Why is there a fee at all?** Because someone has to run the keeper, pay for two independent score feeds and run the randomness provider on every pool, including pools created from other people's clients. That is what the platform's 5% pays for. The other 5% goes to whoever created the pool, so bringing a grid to your bar is worth doing. The creator may add up to 5% more, shown to buyers as one total, and 15% is a ceiling written into the program: config can lower it, never raise it. No fee leaves the vault until the pool has paid a prize, so every return is a full return. The arithmetic is in [Fees](../README.md#fees).

**Who decides the score? Why not an oracle network?** The score decides. The keeper posts the official end-of-quarter line score once two independent primary sources agree, with a third on standby, and the program pays the box whose digits match. An oracle network would add a dependency with its own fee and trust model and would still be reading the same handful of sports-data vendors underneath. The platform reads two of them itself and the program bounds what it can do with the result: one quarter at a time, in order, never sooner than fifteen minutes after kickoff or after the previous post, never a payout to any box but the one the score names. If the sources disagree, nothing is paid until a person has looked.

**What happens if the game is delayed, postponed, cancelled or suspended?** Delayed: the pool waits. Postponed or cancelled, even after the draw: every purchase and every sponsorship is returned in full; a postponed game is never carried to its new date, and creators open fresh pools on it. Suspended and not finished: every unpaid prize is split equally across all 25 boxes. A kickoff the league moves before the game (flex scheduling, broadcast or weather) moves the pool's kickoff with it, within limits the program checks. The full table is [If a game doesn't go to plan](../README.md#if-a-game-doesnt-go-to-plan).

**How are the digits drawn, and how do I check them?** When the 25th box sells, the pool locks and the keeper opens a commit-reveal variable: it publishes a hash of a secret seed and an end slot, the chain's hash of that slot is sampled afterwards, the seed is revealed, and the value is the hash of both. The keeper cannot know the slot hash in advance and nobody else knows the seed. The pool records the commit before the end slot and the draw refuses any value that does not match it, so there are no re-rolls. The digit assignment is written on the pool account and emitted as an event; the app shows it, and anyone can recompute it from the chain. See [Randomness](../README.md#randomness).

**Do I have to claim my winnings?** No. The settlement transaction that posts the quarter's score pays the winning wallet in the same breath. Returns and splits are pushed the same way. There is nothing to claim, request or dispute.

**Who holds the money?** A vault owned by the program, one per pool. Only the program moves it, by the rules in the deployed version, which is published here with verifiable builds. Not the team, not the creator, not the keeper. Every payout is recorded on the pool and in an event with a link to its transaction.

**What can the team do, and what can't it?** The team's multisig can adjust config within hard-coded ceilings, mark a game postponed, cancelled or suspended, cancel a pool before its first settlement, replace a failed draw with a public event, and publish new program versions. It cannot move money to itself, redirect a payout, or change a pool's price, split or fees after creation. Every admin action is an explicit, documented instruction that emits an event; there is no quiet exception anywhere in the program.

**What if MyBarPool stopped running?** A pool still unresolved 30 days after its game's original scheduled kickoff can be reclaimed by its buyers directly from the program, with no permission needed. A running keeper never lets a pool get there, so the rule is a guarantee rather than a feature anyone expects to use.

**Which tokens can a pool use?** SOL, SKR and ORE at launch, one token per pool, with price ladders set in config. The list is a config value and can grow without a program upgrade.

**Why 25 boxes and not 100?** Because 25 fills. Each axis carries two digits per lane, so every box covers 4 of the 100 last-digit pairs, every box has the same 4% chance per quarter, and a full grid always has a winner. A hundred boxes is the Super Bowl party version; twenty-five is the one a bar sells before kickoff on a Thursday night.

**Can I run a pool just for my bar?** Yes. The program supports `link` pools, gated by a key carried in the invite link or a printable QR code, and `allowlist` pools, gated by a list of wallets. Same fees, draw and settlement as public pools. The first version of the app creates public pools only and exposes private pools later; other clients can use them from day one.

**Can I build my own client on it?** Yes. Everything that matters is enforced by the program, not the client, so any frontend, bot or integration gets the same fees, limits, draw and settlement, and the keeper serves every pool on the program whoever created it. A client earns the creator share on pools it creates, or an integrator share on pools its users create. See [Building on the program](../README.md#building-on-the-program).

**Is MyBarPool affiliated with the NFL?** No. Team names are used only to say which game a pool is on; teams are shown by abbreviation and colors on a generic helmet, never by a club logo. See [TRADEMARKS.md](../TRADEMARKS.md).

**Where are the exact rules?** The README has them in tables; [ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md) has the reasoning and the decisions log; [PROGRAM.md](PROGRAM.md) is the mechanical specification the program is built and audited against.
